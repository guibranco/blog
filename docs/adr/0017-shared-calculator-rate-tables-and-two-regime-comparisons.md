# A rate table that more than one Calculator reads lives once, in `_data/calculators/shared/`

ADR-0015 put every number a Calculator needs in `_data/calculators/<name>.yml`, next to the tax year and the sources it was checked against. The second and third Calculators broke that one-file symmetry: the CLT × PJ comparison needs the same INSS, IRRF, Lei 15.270, FGTS, 13º and holiday tables the Ireland × Brazil comparison already carried, and the CLT × PJ Post renders those tables in its prose from the data file. Copying the block would have meant two files to edit every January, with an audit that does not read `_data/calculators/` and so nothing to catch the copies drifting apart.

**A table that more than one Calculator reads lives in `_data/calculators/shared/<topic>.yml`, and each Calculator's own file keeps only what is its own.** `shared/brazil-clt.yml` holds the Brazilian CLT payslip tables with their own `tax_year`, `updated` and `sources`; `net-salary-ie-br.yml` keeps the Irish side, the exchange rate and its labels; `clt-vs-pj-br.yml` keeps the Simples Nacional annexes, the pró-labore rules, the employer-cost premises and its labels. An include reads the shared file with `site.data.calculators.shared["brazil-clt"]`, hands it to its script as a second JSON block (`data-calc-shared`), lists the shared sources after its own and prints the later of the two `updated` dates in its eyebrow. UI copy is never shared: it is Post content in the Calculator's own voice (ADR-0015).

**What every Calculator include repeats is a partial under `_includes/calculators/shared/`, inlined into the include at build time.** `style.html` is the stylesheet; `lib.js` the band maths (`progressive`, `bracket`), the bisection (`solve`), the DOM builders (`el`, `row`, `headline`), the formatters and the `setup`/`start` wiring every init repeats; `brazil-clt.js` the CLT payslip (`inss`, `irrf`, `payslip`); `brazil-clt-tables.html` the CLT static tables. The HTML partials go in with a plain `{% include %}`; the JavaScript ones with `{% capture %}{% include %}{% endcapture %}` and `{{ _lib | strip }}` inside the include's own `<script>`, so the script keeps no blank line and no global. The rendered page is what ADR-0015 promised — one root element carrying its markup, style and script — but the source carries each of those once, which is what Sonar's duplication gate on new code measures. Adding a Calculator to a page still touches nothing outside its include.

**The CLT × PJ Calculator compares a CLT salary with a PJ invoice over a year and solves for the invoice that breaks even.** The CLT year is the one ADR-0015 already models — eleven regular months, the holiday month with its third, the 13º taxed on its own — plus the meal voucher and health plan the employer pays and the FGTS it deposits: the "package". The PJ month is the invoice less the DAS (Anexo III when the pró-labore is at least 28% of the invoice, Anexo V otherwise, at the effective rate of the band the invoice times the months invoiced falls in), the partner's 11% INSS on a pró-labore floored at the minimum wage, the IRRF on that pró-labore with the same Lei 15.270 reduction, the accountant and the fees; the year is that month times eleven or twelve invoices. The break-even invoice comes from bisection, and the same card shows what the CLT costs the employer — the salary plus monthly provisions for the 13º and the holiday month, all carrying the 26.8% charges, the FGTS and the provision for its 40% fine, plus the benefits — because the two multipliers bound the negotiation.

**The Contrato × Recibos Verdes Calculator models Portugal's two arrangements for tax year 2026 as annual liabilities, not payslips.** Contract: fourteen payments, 11% Segurança Social on all of it, IRS by the nine brackets of art. 68.º on the gross less the specific deduction (8.54 × IAS, or the contributions when higher), the solidarity surtax above €80,000. Recibos verdes: Segurança Social at 21.4% on 70% of the invoiced amount, capped at 12 × IAS a month, never below €20 and exempt in the first year of activity; IRS under the simplified regime with the 0.75 coefficient of the art. 151.º professions, halved in the first year and cut by a quarter in the second, the 15% expense rule of art. 31.º n.º 13 and the deduction of contributions above 10% of gross of art. 31.º n.º 2; the contabilista as a cost. Withholding at 23% and IVA at 23% are printed as information only, because one is an advance on the same tax and the other passes through.

## Consequences

- A new tax year for Brazil is one edit to `shared/brazil-clt.yml`; both Calculators and the CLT × PJ Post's prose tables change together. Ireland and Portugal stay in their own files.
- A `shared/` file has the same `tax_year`, `updated` and `sources` shape as a Calculator file, so a reviewer checks it the same way; it has no include and no labels of its own.
- The Ireland × Brazil include changed shape (a second JSON block, a merged source list, the partials) but not its calculation.
- A change to a partial reaches every Calculator, so each one is checked after it: the pure functions in Node against known payslips, the built page in headless Chrome for what the script rendered.
- Deleting a Calculator is still deleting its include and its own data file; a shared file stays while anything reads it.
- Each page says what it leaves out. Brazil: ISS by city, RAT and FAP by activity, Lucro Presumido, overtime, bonuses, unemployment insurance and the FGTS fine on dismissal. Portugal: tax credits, joint taxation, IRS Jovem, the subsistence minimum, meal allowance, organised accounts and the ±25% adjustment of the Segurança Social base — items that weigh the same on both sides of the comparison.
- The Ireland × Brazil Calculator stays where it is (parts 2 of "Trabalhar fora" and the Dublin Post) and is not embedded in further Posts; the CLT × PJ Post embeds the CLT × PJ Calculator instead.
